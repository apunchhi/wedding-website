## 2. Our Story — CONFIRMED (from AA)

We met in 2010 when we both lived in the same apartment building in Mumbai: Quantum Park.

Amar’s sister, Gopika and Ananya would often hang out after school. On one particular afternoon, Amar barged into Gopika’s room to share his recent obsession with Rubik’s cubes. While Gopika was mortified by her brother’s presence, Ananya couldn’t help but think that the curly-haired boy, sharing his love for puzzles, was incredibly cute.

In the following months, Ananya developed her own obsession with speed cubing that crystallized her first impression of Amar. Amar, far too obsessed with shaving off seconds from his solve time, would fail to remember the day he met his future wife.

Over the next decade, the Punchhi and Tinaikar families would move around the world. Our moms continued to stay in touch, but we never crossed paths.

Fast forward to July 2022, NYC. A birthday party for our friend Ameera’s Dad, Salim, brought us together again. Ananya reminded Amar that they had met before, and we proceeded to spend several hours talking. Despite this promising start, we still failed to exchange numbers.

This time, Amar would make sure to remember their meeting.

He texted Ameera, who offered to pass along Ananya’s number. But, not wanting to be too forward with a family friend, Amar responded:

*“It’s okay. I’ll just leave this one up to fate.”*

In February 2023, Ananya attended a [@drinksfirst](https://www.instagram.com/drinksfirst/) event hosted by her friend, Ariana. By coincidence, Amar decided to go out with his friends to that very same event.

In a sea of people, music, and LED lights, we ran into each other…again! We finally exchanged numbers and the rest is history.

In November 2025, Amar proposed to Ananya on the rooftop of Quantum Park, and after many years, fate finally got its way.

### Visa research — not on the site

The site now uses the planners' short visa answer. This research is kept for
reference only. Do not send people to third-party “India e-visa” mills.

- **Indian passport:** no visa.
- **OCI:** lifelong multi-entry visa; travel with current foreign passport + OCI card. [MEA OCI scheme](https://www.mea.gov.in/overseas-citizenship-of-india-scheme)
- **Most others:** e-Tourist Visa at [indianvisaonline.gov.in/evisa/tvoa.html](https://indianvisaonline.gov.in/evisa/tvoa.html) only. Official purpose list includes recreation / sightseeing and “casual visit to meet friends and relatives” (Q1). Apply min 4 days before arrival; 1-year/5-year up to 120 days ahead, 30-day visa only 30 days ahead (Q5). Passport 6 months’ validity at application + 2 blank pages; return/onward ticket (Q1). ETA must show GRANTED; carry a printout; biometrics on arrival. Fee country-specific, non-refundable, + ~3% bank charge (Q3, Q13). Kids need their own passport and e-Visa (Q12).
- **Entry:** designated airports include **Chandigarh**, Delhi, Mumbai, etc. Cannot enter on e-Visa via undesignated land borders (Q11, Q23).
- **VoA:** Japan, South Korea, UAE (UAE only if they previously held an Indian e-Visa or paper visa) — and only at six airports (Bengaluru, Chennai, Delhi, Hyderabad, Kolkata, Mumbai), **not Chandigarh**. [VoA page](https://indianvisaonline.gov.in/visa/visa-on-arrival.html)
- **Nepal / Bhutan:** visa not required (standard GoI policy).
- **Pakistani passport or Pakistani origin** (parent/grandparent born in or permanently resident in Pakistan): **not eligible for e-Visa**; regular visa at an Indian Mission (Q1).
- Scam warning: no official “express fee”; only the .gov.in portal (Q3). Embassy of India, Washington repeats this.

- **Climatology (corroborates, does not replace):** Chandigarh November mean max ~26°C (79°F), mean min ~12°C (54°F); ~6–8 mm rain; ~1 wet day; driest month. Late month (19–20 Nov) is the cooler half (Weather & Climate: highs fall from ~29°C early Nov to ~24°C late Nov; nights ~10–15°C).
